Clotr: clot your data leaks

A helper in your web browser. When you type to an AI chat like ChatGPT, Gemini or Copilot, or in an email or chat app you switched it on for, it notices private things before you send them, like a password, your phone number, your address or a card number.

A note shows in the corner of the chat, like "Heads up, your message contains a phone number."

  • Hide it takes it out of your message.
  • Leave it in if you meant to share it.
  • Not sure? Hide it. You can always type it again.

Tourniquet is on

It never sends anything anywhere and never saves what you type. It works in AI chats, and on your email or a chat app only if someone switched it on there.

Real support never asks for your password, a code texted to you, or your card. If someone does, stop and call the person below.

Something wrong, or a warning that shouldn't be there? Tell Clotr's maker at github.com/clotr-app/clotr/issues (it's public: never write the real detail there).

Not on the phone, not in a text, not in a chat, not even when they say it's urgent.

The code your bank or an app texts you

The 3 numbers on the back of your card

Gift card numbers
No real business or government agency asks to be paid with gift cards.
A code that lets someone into your computer
Real tech companies don't call or text you about a problem with your computer.
Your passwords and backup codes
They're the keys to your accounts. Nobody else needs them.
Bank, card or Social Security numbers, to someone who contacted you first
Hang up. Call back on a number you already know.
