What Clotr can reach

    
Nothing outside your browser. Clotr has no server and makes no connections. Your browser enforces this
      rule on Clotr's own pages (read live from the installed copy):

    

    
connect-src 'self' means these pages can only load Clotr's own files: no website, no
      server. The part that watches AI chat boxes runs inside those pages; it contains no code that sends anything, and an
      automated check fails the build if any is ever added.

Known limits

  
    
    
  • Short details can be guessed from their fingerprints by someone who can open this browser's profile on this computer. There aren't that many possible phone numbers, or places a photo could be taken, so a determined person could try them all. Fingerprints keep your details from casual eyes, not from someone with full access to your computer.
    • 
    
  • AI sites may keep your unsent message in this browser. Some, like ChatGPT, save what you're typing so it's still there after a reload. Nothing is sent until you press Send, and hiding something replaces the saved copy too.
    • 
    
  • Clotr can't read pictures. It checks what you type and the text in files you attach (text, PDF, Word, Excel and PowerPoint). In a picture or a screenshot it checks only the file's name and, for a photo, whether the place it was taken is saved inside it. It can't read the words or see what a picture shows, so look pictures over yourself. It doesn't hear voice. If your vault has details, it also reads the AI's reply for 90 seconds after you send, only to notice your own details, and nothing from a reply is kept.
    • 
    
  • A long list sent at once is counted, not kept one by one. Past 10 of the same kind in one go, Clotr keeps one record with a count for the rest, so your history keeps its room. Every count adds up, but the rest have no fingerprint, so Clotr can't tell if one of them comes back later.
    • 
    
  • Clotr only works on the AI chats it knows and on the sites you switched on, and only in this browser.
    • 
    
  • It can miss things and sometimes warn about ordinary text. It's a safety net, not a guarantee.
    • 
    
  • A cover name's underline can show through faint page effects. Clotr hides a cover name's dotted underline while one of the site's own menus or pop-ups is on top of it. A faint effect over the text, like a fade at the bottom of the chat, doesn't count, so the underline can show through it. Nothing is sent, it's only how it looks.
    • 
    
  • Clotr can't see pop-ups around a chat shown inside another page. If a site puts its chat in a frame inside another page, a cover name's underline can show through that page's pop-ups. None of the AI chats Clotr knows works this way today.
