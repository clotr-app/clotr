    What to do now

    • Paid with a gift card? Call the gift card company on the number on the back of the card. Say a scammer got the number and PIN, and ask for your money back.
    • Let someone into your computer? Update your security software and run a scan. Then change your passwords and turn on two-step sign-in.
    • Gave a password or a sign-in code? Change that password, and turn on two-step sign-in.
    • Someone offers to get your money back for a fee? That's a scam too.
    • In the US, report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. Gave your Social Security number? IdentityTheft.gov gives you a recovery plan.

    Clotr doesn't ask what happened, and keeps nothing about it.

  4. Make the warnings bigger

    Bigger text and bigger buttons in Clotr's notes, easier to read and to click.

  5. Ask before personal details go out

    When a message holds a phone number, an address or a birthday, Clotr stops and asks before it's sent. Passwords and keys always get a warning.

  6. Also on their email and chat apps

    Tick the ones they use. The browser then asks once, for just those sites. There, Clotr warns and hides like on an AI chat, and never reads anyone's replies.

  7. Lock the settings with a PIN

    The PIN stops accidental changes. It isn't a real lock, since anyone who can remove Clotr from the browser can reset it.

How it works

  • Clotr asks before these details go out, and they can always send it anyway.
  • Every Clotr screen shows them that Tourniquet is on, and what it does.
  • Their history stays on this computer, and they can clear it without the PIN.
  • Clotr sends nothing anywhere.

What they'll see

A note in the corner of the AI chat when their message has something private in it, with a button to hide it. With Ask before personal details on, Clotr asks before one goes out. Clotr itself never sends anything anywhere.
