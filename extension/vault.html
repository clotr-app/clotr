Hi, I'm Alex.

I made Clotr after I noticed how much I was telling AI chats without a second thought, I immediately wanted a Tony Stark style "Suit of armor around the world" to exist. So, here we are. It reads what you type into an AI chat, right here on your computer, and speaks up before something private goes out. It's free, it doesn't track you, and it never sends anything anywhere.

My main goal is privacy from big AI, for everyone. Clotr is also my 4D chess move. Building it is how I'm learning software engineering as a whole, and how I turn what I'm learning at university into something people can actually use.

Clotr has absolutely NO AI, it defeats the point.

The more you use Clotr, the more you report bugs, the better it gets. For free.

It only runs on AI chats, and on any email or chat app you switch on yourself. It warns you before a password, a key or a personal detail goes out, and then it's up to you to take it out or send it anyway.

  • Your browser asks you first for every email or chat app you add, so Clotr only ever covers the sites you've chosen.
  • There are no Clotr servers, so nothing you type ever leaves this computer.
  • It never saves what you type, only a short note of what it found, like “Phone number · chatgpt.com · hidden,” which you can see from its toolbar button.
  • By default it only warns, with a note in the corner, and the message stays yours to send.
  • Once you add your details below, it also reads the AI's replies, so it can tell you if one brings up something you didn't type, and you can turn that off in Settings.
  • It only works in this browser, so be just as careful with AI apps on your phone or built into other programs.

Tell Clotr a little about yourself and it'll watch for these in AI chats, however they're written. It saves a one-way fingerprint of each item, never the item itself, so anyone opening this extension's storage would find nothing readable. It takes about two minutes. One item per line, and skip anything you like.

First and last, maiden name, nicknames. Up to 4 words per line.

Kids, partner, parents: the names you'd mention when asking for advice.

Employer, school, or your own business.

Home, work, mailing. "St" and "Street", "123" and "one twenty three" all match.

Any format; digits are what count.

Type your number, like AB-123456: Clotr saves its format and a scrambled code of it (never the number), so it catches any ID in that format and knows which one is yours. Short numbers can be guessed from the code by someone with full access to this computer; to save only the format, type it with # for a digit and @ for a letter, like AB-######.

Client names, project codenames, internal terms, or a format like EMP-#####.
