# Clotr Commitments

Clotr is free, runs on your device, collects nothing and watches nobody. This page is about the people who help build it, and what I'm allowed to charge for. I wrote it so I can be held to it, not just so it reads nice.

## 1. What you contribute stays free and open

- Anything you contribute to Clotr's free tools is licensed under AGPL-3.0-or-later, same as the rest of the project.
- You keep your copyright. You sign off your commits (the DCO) and that's it. How it works is in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).
- I will never sell your contribution. Your work is yours.
- I'll credit you in the release notes and in a CONTRIBUTORS file unless you'd rather I didn't.

## 2. What I may charge for

I'm a solo developer and I pay for Clotr out of pocket. I'd like it to help pay for itself without ever touching your work. Money has never been the goal, and it never will be. The rule I'm holding myself to: **if a contributor's code is needed for it to work, it isn't for sale.**

I may charge for:

- My own time and know-how: helping a small office get set up, privacy check-ups, training and support.
- Separate tools and services I write myself, with no outside code in them, that sit on top of Clotr and talk to it through a documented and public  interface.

Donations and sponsorships are gifts, not sales. How I use them is in [FUNDING.md](FUNDING.md).

## 3. What I will never do

- Charge for the Clotr extension, or for anyone's everyday protection. IE, 'thank you for your taxes'.
- Lock any warning or detection in the free tools behind a payment (See above).
- Collect, upload or sell what people type, or anything about them.
- Build anything on top of Clotr that reports on a person to someone else, like an employer. Nobody should be watched through it.

## 4. The paid tools stay separate

The team and developer tools I sell live apart from Clotr's free code, and are written only by me, with no outside contributions. They don't copy Clotr's code. They only work with it through its public interface, so the free tools stay checkable for network code and for every promise above.

## 5. The name and the logo

I look after the Clotr name and logo so a copy can't pass as the real thing, or claim these promises when it doesn't keep them. The code is free to fork and improve. A fork needs its own name, except as section 7 describes.

## 6. Earlier contributors and the previous agreement

Before this, contributors agreed to a Contributor License Agreement (version 1.0) that gave me the right to license their work under other licenses. I won't use that right. I'd rather earn trust than hold a power I don't plan to use.

## 7. If I stop

If there's no release and no reply to issues or security reports for 12 months, Clotr counts as inactive. Then:

- Anyone can pick it up and continue it, and may call it "Clotr (community continuation)"
- Someone I trust already has what's needed to keep this from vanishing. The details stay private for security.
- Whoever continues it is held to these same commitments, in perpetuity.

## 8. Changing these commitments

I can widen these, never narrow them. Any change gets announced before it takes effect, and contributions made earlier stay covered by the commitments as they were when you made them.

I'm a solo STUDENT developer, not a lawyer. If a line here is unclear or unfair to you, open an issue and tell me. I'm a dude, we're all dudes, and I'm open to just talking.
