# Scam messages people receive, in English: texts, emails, chat messages and what a caller said, built from the US FTC's
# scenarios (a bank's fraud team, the government, tech support, a grandchild in trouble, romance, a prize, a parcel, a
# toll, a job, a buyer's code, the paste-a-command trick). Every one is made up: phone numbers are 555-01xx, links and
# emails use example.com or example.invalid, and every person, bank and shop is invented. Some have typos and number
# words on purpose, the way real ones do.
# Each message starts with one label line, the warning signs Clotr's "Is this a scam?" page must find in it
# (scam-signs.js), then the message:
#   [signs: asks_code, press_rush, pret_bank]
# Every listed sign must be found, and a sign the label doesn't list is a wrong one (tests/scam-signs.test.js, and the
# scorecard). "Mentions your own details" (pret_knows_you) needs the person's vault, so it's tested on its own.
# Messages are separated by lines of "---".
